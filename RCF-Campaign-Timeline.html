Review windows are the schedule's single biggest dependency

The 1–3 day windows above are given as a point of reference, and they're what the rest of the schedule is built on. The delays the project is currently carrying come from feedback generally not arriving inside them, and that is the reason two weeks are being added to the end of the engagement. Three days should be the high end rather than the default, and two rounds should only be used where genuinely needed — most of these deliverables won't require a second. What that means in practice: all stakeholder feedback needs to be gathered and returned as one consolidated response inside the stated window. If it isn't, dates move again, and there's no further runway to add at the back end.

Where the postcard sits in this

The postcard is primarily a general awareness move, and it shouldn't be measured strictly on conversions into the three-part guide. Its job is getting RCF's name out in the community, with a convenient DAF-related call to action for anyone who happens to be interested when it arrives. Mail like this is strong for brand awareness, and awareness built now converts into relationships later — often well outside the window in which the piece mailed.

5

How we lift consultations

A consultation is the high-commitment step in this funnel. These are the changes going in now, and the levers beyond them.

In flight now

  • A rebuilt consultation page. Credibility elements, easier navigation, one clear call to action, and the form on the page rather than a click away.
  • An immediate consultation offer after guide sign-up. The thank-you page carries a button straight through to the consultation request, which also gives clean event tracking.
  • A Google Voice number forwarding to RCF's main line, routing to voicemail when nobody picks up, with messages transcribed and emailed. Captures prospects who would rather call than complete a form.
  • A softer ask. "A 20-minute call, no obligation" rather than "consultation," which carries weight of its own.

A consultation remains a heavy ask

Requesting a consultation is a high-commitment step, on a financial topic, from a cold audience. The changes above remove friction, but they don't remove the weight of the ask itself. These are the next levers we'd recommend, most of them suited to a following phase of work.
  • Extend the series from three emails to five or six. The series is where conversion happens, and three emails is a short runway for a decision this size. The added emails would carry the credibility indicators, the motivators, and the items below that don't fit on a page — testimonials, the DAF explainer, the advisor-facing material.
  • An on-page video explaining what a DAF is — the biggest comprehension barrier, and video handles it better than copy.
  • Real testimonials from existing fund holders. Nothing substitutes for it on a financial page.
  • Ministry and organizational associations shown on the page. For this audience, who RCF stands alongside carries more weight than any claim RCF makes about itself.
  • An extended branded PDF a prospect can forward to a spouse or a financial advisor. These decisions are rarely made alone.
  • Educational video ads in a future Meta update, warming the audience before the ask.
  • Ongoing educational social content, so the ads aren't the only place RCF explains this.
